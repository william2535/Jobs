# Checklist standards reference

Public sources checked on **14 November 2026**. This is a source register for developing UK discipline-specific visit checklists and reviewed document templates. It is not the text of a standard, a complete inspection schedule, or a validated certificate specification.

The identifiers and publication states below were visible on the linked publisher or certification-body pages. Catalogue states can change, and different contract, installation and certification-scheme editions may apply. Record the actual applicable standard/edition for the job before applying a reviewed template; do not silently substitute an edition because a reference appears here.

“Security” and “Custom” are app classifications, not identified technical standards. They may describe mixed systems or a special scope. Their checklists must retain the recorded scope and cannot inherit an Intruder, CCTV or Access control certificate declaration merely from the label.

## Implications for the Jobs checklist

Discipline and job type can select useful recording prompts. A prompt marked done records an engineer action; it does not establish conformity with every requirement of a standard. Device outcomes, measured values and units, defects, omissions, restricted access, work boundaries and the condition left must come from actual records. They must not be inferred from drawing status, a completed job, or a completed checklist.

Maintenance, installation, small works, takeover and call-out records have different purposes. An installation job does not automatically authorize a design or commissioning declaration. Small works must identify the affected work rather than declare conformity of the entire existing installation. A takeover or call-out may require a findings report without any whole-system declaration. Selecting a certificate ultimately needs the actual responsibilities, premises and system scope, as well as the job category.

Quick checklist summaries can reduce repeated entry and prefill known site/job/equipment/drawing references. They cannot replace the complete applicable inspection schedule, manufacturer instructions, competent judgement or scheme requirements. A valid findings record may contain defects and limitations; “make every device satisfactory” must never be used as a shortcut to completion.

## Supplied fire maintenance report reference

The separate [fire maintenance form](fire-maintenance.md) derives 21 prompt types from pages 2–4 of the PDF supplied by the user: eight system questions, seven panel questions and six battery questions. The reference also informed the A4 report arrangement. It is an example of a visit record, not the full text of BS 5839 or a validated set of compliance rules. Panel and battery checks are added explicitly for the equipment actually checked; the software does not assume they exist from the selected discipline.

The example's three-year battery check is not treated as a universal replacement limit. Jobs asks whether service life was checked against the actual manufacturer's criteria, and records the engineer's answer and readings. It does not infer acceptable voltage ranges, capacity or service life. The sample's customer information, logos, accreditation marks and terms are not copied into application fixtures or report templates.

## Generic visit certificates and standards-specific forms

These public catalogues and explanations establish identifiers and broad scope. They do not provide a complete, rights-cleared certificate template or the complete normative field, test, declaration and exception rules. No licensed full standard or specialist-reviewed certificate template was validated during this research. No proprietary standards text or third-party branded certificate was copied into Jobs.

Jobs now offers [generic visit certificates](certificates.md) with explicit review, numbering, immutable snapshots and retained revisions. This does not validate a standards-specific declaration or grant scheme approval. Before offering a certificate as a validated standards-specific form, its template needs:

- An explicit jurisdiction, premises/system scope, standard and edition, including relevant amendments and certification-scheme requirements.
- A rights-cleared template, versioned field mapping, required tests and evidence, declarations and handling of variations, defects, omissions and limited access, reviewed against the complete applicable requirements by a competent specialist.
- Recorded issuer and engineer responsibilities. A client acknowledgement must remain distinct from an engineer's technical declaration; software cannot grant scheme membership, competence or authority to issue branded certificates.
- An immutable document record linked to the supporting job and drawing revisions, with document numbering and retained revision/correction history.

The existing service reports remain explicitly labelled **Draft service report**. The separate issued visit certificate is limited to the engineer's entered scope and declaration; neither a completed checklist nor a generated PDF establishes whole-system compliance. The existing [build brief](BUILD_BRIEF.md#certificate-generation) records the validation boundary for standards-specific compliance documents.

## Retrieval boundaries

The source pages above were retrieved directly from BSI and NSI using ordinary public HTTPS requests. BSI catalogue metadata and NSI public summaries were consulted; licensed standards, subscriber-only technical bulletins and scheme forms were not accessed. BSIA's public standards-overview URL returned an access-denied page, so it is not used as evidence. No assertion of a current edition is made for identifiers where an edition was not verified. Future template work should recheck publisher status and the applicable contract/scheme transition requirements.
